# Cloudflare's Free Tier Powers Entire Applications

As of 14 October 2026, Cloudflare's free tier is large enough to run a complete website and app, but "every single free thing" cannot be switched on from one bare account, because the free tier is a catalog of metered budgets guarded by three gates. The budgets are generous where a small product needs them: **unlimited static-asset requests**, a Worker API capped at **100,000 requests a day and 10 ms of CPU per invocation** ([Workers Limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/#daily-requests)), D1 with **5 million rows read and 100,000 rows written a day** ([D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)), SQLite Durable Objects, KV, Queues, Workflows, Workers AI at **10,000 Neurons a day** ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)), Vectorize, AI Gateway, unlimited inbound Email Routing, SFU/TURN at **1,000 GB a month**, unmetered DDoS protection, Universal SSL, Turnstile, Zaraz at **1,000,000 events a month** and a 50-user Zero Trust plan. Most daily budgets reset at **00:00 UTC** and fail with an error rather than a bill, but R2, Logpush, Web3 gateways, Basin and (from 1 November) AI Search bill automatically once a card is attached. The three gates are a domain you own (needed for DNS, CDN, WAF, Rules and Email Routing; Registrar sells at cost and no free domain turned up in any source), a payment method on file (R2, Zero Trust onboarding, Cloudflare for SaaS, Basin), and the $5 Workers Paid plan (seven premium Workers AI models, email to arbitrary recipients, Containers, Artifacts). Video hosting, load balancing, custom error pages, Snippets and regex rules are paid outright, many free items are still betas that can start billing (Secrets Store, Workers AI LoRA, Traces, Issues, Mesh, Workers VPC, Cloud Connector), and two billing switches reach free builds soon: AI Search on **1 November** and pooled Observability on **1 December**, which replaces the 200,000-event Workers Logs allowance with a shared 0.5 GB a day ([Observability pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/observability/pricing/)). The workable strategy is static-first, cache-heavy and budget-aware, so that a daily cap degrades one feature instead of taking the site down.

## Hard daily stops, not surprise bills, define the free tier

The phrase "use every free thing" hides three questions that Cloudflare's documentation answers product by product: how large the allowance is, what happens when it runs out, and what must exist before the product can be switched on. Read together, Cloudflare's pages show that the answers cluster into a few patterns, and those patterns matter more to a builder than any single product page. The core developer platform fails closed. Past **100,000 requests a day**, Workers return Error 1027, and each route chooses whether to fail open (bypass the Worker) or fail closed ([Workers Limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/#daily-requests)). KV, Hyperdrive and Durable Objects fail the exhausted type of operation until the 00:00 UTC reset ([KV pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/platform/pricing/), [Hyperdrive pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/platform/pricing/), [Durable Objects pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)), and D1 joined them on 2026-09-01, when queries on Free accounts began failing once the daily row limits were exceeded ([D1 changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-01-d1-free-tier-limit-enforcement/)). Workers AI answers HTTP 429 with error 3036 and cannot bill a Free account at all ([Workers AI errors](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/errors/)); Browser Run returns 429 until the next UTC day ([Browser Run limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/limits/)); Images rejects new transformations with error 9422 but never bills ([Images pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/images/pricing/)); and Zaraz switches itself off until the next billing cycle unless usage billing is enabled ([Zaraz pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/zaraz/pricing-info/)). Security and Rules quotas are configuration caps rather than meters: a Free zone gets five WAF custom rules, and the documentation describes no billing path for a sixth ([WAF custom rules](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waf/custom-rules/)).

A second group bills automatically against a card. R2 charges overage with no documented hard cap (the pricing page's own asset-hosting example bills $104.40 for 290 million Class B operations above the allowance) ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)); Logpush meters anything beyond 25 GB of internal exports, 25 GB of external exports and 1 GB of transformations per account per month ([Logpush pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/logs/logpush/pricing/)); Cloudflare for SaaS charges $0.10 per custom hostname after the first 100 ([plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/plans/)); Basin Catalog and SQL have invoiced overage since 2026-08-03 ([Basin Catalog pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin-catalog/platform/pricing/)); and AI Search begins usage-based billing on 2026-11-01 ([AI Search limits and pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-search/platform/limits-pricing/)). Budget alerts exist but only notify, and only for pay-as-you-go accounts; hard spending caps are slated for early availability in the fourth quarter of 2026 ([Enterprise-for-all update](https://blog.cloudflare.com/enterprise-for-all-update/)). A third group publishes limits but never says what happens when a Free account crosses them: Queues, Workflows, Workers Analytics Engine and Vectorize ([Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/), [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/), [Analytics Engine pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/pricing/), [Vectorize pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/vectorize/platform/pricing/)). Every design should therefore catch send errors from those services and degrade to a fallback.

The budget that binds hardest is the shared Worker-request pool. Pages Functions and Workflow instance creation draw on the same **100,000 requests a day** as ordinary HTTP traffic, and a Cron Trigger invocation counts as a request ([Pages Functions pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/functions/pricing/), [Workers Pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)). Requests for static assets are free and unlimited because only requests that reach a Worker count ([Static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). That asymmetry is the central design rule of a zero-dollar build: put pages, scripts and images in static assets, and spend Worker requests only on the API.

The card question has a firm answer in only some places. Cloudflare's Zero Trust setup guide says the payment step is still required on the Free plan although nothing is charged ([Zero Trust setup](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/setup/)); the Basin pricing pages require an R2 subscription and a linked card ([Basin Catalog pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin-catalog/platform/pricing/)); and Cloudflare for SaaS asks for payment information when it is enabled ([SaaS enable](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/enable/)). R2's own pages say only that you complete a checkout flow to add an R2 subscription ([R2 get started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/get-started/)), the generic billing docs say a primary payment method is required to purchase products ([billing profile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/billing/get-started/create-billing-profile/)), and third-party guides report that a card is requested before the first bucket can be created. The reasonable position is to plan on attaching a card for R2 and Zero Trust and to verify in the dashboard before relying on either claim.

### Terms of service fence what "free" may serve

Free does not mean unrestricted. On Free, Pro and Business zones, Cloudflare's service-specific terms require paid services (the Developer Platform, Images or Stream) for video and other large files, and let Cloudflare limit CDN access when a disproportionate share of traffic is such files ([Application Services terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-application-services/)); the Tunnel FAQ repeats the restriction for public hostname routes ([Tunnels FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/faq/cloudflare-tunnels-faq/)). The Developer Platform terms let Cloudflare limit storage or request volume without notice if usage burdens the network, and change workers.dev and pages.dev subdomains with at least a week's notice ([Developer Platform terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/)). Individual products carry their own fences: r2.dev is for non-production use ([R2 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/)), Quick Tunnels are for testing ([Quick Tunnels](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/get-started/quick-tunnels/)), Radar API data is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 ([Radar docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/radar/)), and the 1.1.1.1 resolver and Always Online carry no SLA ([1.1.1.1 terms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1/terms-of-use/), [Always Online](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/how-to/always-online/)).

## Static assets are unlimited, but every dynamic call spends a 100,000-request budget

The developer platform needs only a Cloudflare account, and its free allowances dictate the architecture. Static-asset requests on Workers are free and unlimited and never invoke the Worker, so a site or single-page app served as assets can absorb unlimited traffic while only dynamic routes spend the **100,000-request, 10 ms-CPU** daily budget ([Static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). Two settings keep that promise: `run_worker_first = ["/api/*"]` confines Worker invocations to API paths, and a compatibility date of 2025-04-01 or later lets browser navigations in single-page mode skip the Worker entirely ([SPA routing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/routing/single-page-application/)). The Free-plan traps are that a `run_worker_first` route over the cap returns HTTP 429 instead of falling back to assets, and that enabling Workers Cache on the asset-serving Worker makes normally free asset requests bill as Worker requests ([Static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/), [Workers Cache](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/cache/)). Cloudflare's Pages landing page now tells readers to start new projects on Workers, and Pages lacks Cron Triggers, Workers Logs, Previews and the Rate Limiting binding, so the tables treat Pages as legacy ([Pages docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/), [migration guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/migration-guides/migrate-from-pages/)).

### D1 and KV supply a free data layer, while R2 adds a subscription

The data layer is where daily budgets become visible. KV's **1,000 writes a day** (about 0.7 a minute) rules it out for counters, sessions and logs, and its 60-second-plus propagation lag between locations rules it out for read-after-write flows ([KV pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/platform/pricing/), [How KV works](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/)). D1's true budget is rows scanned, not rows returned, so fifty scans of an unindexed 100,000-row table spend the entire **5 million-row** daily read allowance, and each index adds a written row per indexed write ([D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)). Ten databases of 500 MB each cap D1 at 5 GB, which makes per-tenant databases impractical on Free ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/)). R2 breaks the hard-stop pattern because overage is billed, and Hyperdrive is a pooler rather than a database: it needs a Postgres or MySQL host outside Cloudflare, so a Cloudflare-only build skips it.

### Durable Objects, Queues and Workflows share the daily-budget logic

Durable Objects reached Free on 2025-04-07 as SQLite-backed classes only, and since 2026-07-09 an account without an existing key-value namespace cannot create the older key-value backend ([DO free-tier changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-durable-objects-free-tier/), [restriction](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-07-09-restrict-new-kv-backed-namespaces/)). Queues joined Free on 2026-02-04. At three operations per message (write, read, delete) plus one read per retry, the **10,000-operation** daily allowance carries only about 3,300 end-to-end messages, and event subscriptions from other products and R2 notifications draw on the same budget ([Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/)). Workflows' 3,000 daily steps carry about 300 runs of a ten-step workflow, and each instance start also spends a Worker request ([Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)). The Durable Objects documentation is internally inconsistent on two Free figures, per-object storage (10 GB in the table, 1 GB in the FAQ) and CPU per invocation (30 s in the DO table, 10 ms in the Workers limits), so plan with 1 GB and 10 ms ([DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)).

## Ten thousand daily Neurons fund a small AI feature, and video stays on the paid side

AI is where the free tier is simultaneously most useful and most volatile. Workers AI's allowance is one pool of **10,000 Neurons a day**, shared by every model and task and reset at 00:00 UTC; a Free account cannot buy more, and Workers Paid adds $0.011 per 1,000 Neurons ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). Converting the published per-model rates (my arithmetic, assuming 500 input and 300 output tokens per chat and ignoring hidden reasoning tokens), the pool buys roughly **2,600 chats a day on granite-4.0-h-micro, 870 on llama-3.2-3b, 730 on glm-4.7-flash, 280 on llama-4-scout and 130 on llama-3.3-70b**, or about 3,300 500-token chunks on bge-base embeddings, about 170 FLUX schnell 1024-pixel images, or about four hours of Whisper audio. Because the pool is shared, a mixed day of 200 small-model chats, 1,000 query embeddings and 30 images still fits. On 2026-07-28 Cloudflare removed Free access to its most expensive models, and the pricing page now lists seven (kimi-k2.6, kimi-k2.7-code, glm-5.2, glm-5.3, glm-5.3-flash, deepseek-v4-flash-0731 and deepseek-v4-pro-0813) that need Workers Paid or prepaid AI Gateway credits; Free requests to them return HTTP 403 with error 5035 ([changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-07-28-models-require-workers-paid/), [pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). Several Agents SDK examples still default to those paid models and must be swapped for a free one ([Voice agents](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/communication-channels/voice/), [Think](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/harnesses/think/)). A free build pins to unrestricted models such as glm-4.7-flash, gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, gpt-oss-20b and 120b, llama-4-scout, qwen3.8-27b and nemotron-3-120b, and catches 429 and 403 responses.

## A domain you own unlocks DNS, CDN, WAF, SSL and Rules at no recurring cost

A domain is the second gate, and it opens roughly half the catalog. Cloudflare DNS on the Free plan requires the primary (full) setup, in which the registrar's nameservers are switched to two Cloudflare servers; the CNAME (partial) setup that leaves DNS elsewhere is Business or Enterprise only ([Partial setup](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/zone-setups/partial-setup/), [Nameserver options](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/nameservers/nameserver-options/)). The domain must sit one level below a public-suffix TLD, the zone answers DNS-only until ownership verifies, and a domain registered through Cloudflare Registrar must stay on Cloudflare nameservers for the life of the registration ([Primary zone setup](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/zone-setups/full-setup/setup/), [Nameserver options](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/nameservers/nameserver-options/)). Registrar sells at registry cost and the plans page lists it from $7.85 ([Plans](https://www.cloudflare.com/plans/network-cdn.md)), which makes the domain the only unavoidable cash cost of the challenge. Without a zone, no Rules product, Custom Error, Cloud Connector rule, WAF rule or R2 custom domain can be exercised, and workers.dev, pages.dev, r2.dev and trycloudflare.com are the only hostnames available for free ([Rules overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/rules/), [workers.dev](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/routing/workers-dev/)).

### Security and SSL give a strong baseline with deliberately thin edges

A Free zone gets a broad baseline whose edges are thin on purpose. DDoS protection is unmetered, the WAF is a five-rule tool, and bot control splits into a blunt free mode (Bot Fight Mode cannot be bypassed with a Skip action and may challenge API or mobile traffic) and a configurable paid mode (Super Bot Fight Mode, Pro and up) ([Bot Fight Mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/get-started/bot-fight-mode/)). Two interactions deserve a budget line. AI Crawl Control implements a block by creating or updating a WAF custom rule, so each use probably spends one of the five (my inference from two documented facts), and IP Access Rules and Bot Fight Mode run outside the Ruleset Engine, so an IP Access Allow can bypass Bot Fight Mode while a custom-rule Skip cannot ([AI Crawl Control](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/manage-ai-crawlers/), [feature interoperability](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waf/feature-interoperability/)). Under Attack mode, Browser Integrity Check, Security Level and Challenge Passage appear in Security Settings without plan restrictions, though no page states "Free: yes" ([Security settings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/security/settings/)).

## Zero Trust's 50-seat plan and a dozen no-account tools extend the stack at the edges

### A reference build wires the free products in seven layers

The first layer is the foundation. Register or move one domain to Cloudflare DNS with DNSSEC and Universal SSL, add a card only where a product demands it (R2 and Zero Trust), and ship the first deploy on workers.dev so the app is live before the zone is. The second layer is the front end: a static or single-page build in Workers Static Assets, Cache Rules for long-lived assets, Transform Rules for security headers, Early Hints and HTTP/3 left on, and Web Analytics and Zaraz for measurement. The third layer is the API: a Worker on `/api/*`, D1 as the system of record, KV for configuration and cached lookups, one Durable Object per room or session for WebSockets and counters, the Rate Limiting binding plus the single WAF rate-limit rule on the login route, and Turnstile on every form with the `password_leaked` field in a custom rule.

The fourth layer is asynchronous work: Queues for form submissions, webhooks and R2 object-created events, Workflows for multi-step jobs, and Cron Triggers for nightly cleanup, remembering that every cron run spends a Worker request. The fifth layer is AI: Workers AI behind AI Gateway for caching, rate limits and a spend cap, Vectorize or AI Search for retrieval, and Browser Run and Markdown conversion for ingestion. The sixth layer is media and mail: R2 originals served through Images transformations on a fixed width set, STUN, TURN and the SFU for calls, Email Routing with an Email Worker for inbound mail, verified-destination sending for owner alerts, and DMARC Management. The seventh layer is protection and operations: the five custom rules for login and signup challenges, the Free Managed Ruleset, AI bot policies, Access with a Tunnel for the admin panel, Workers Logs, Traces and Issues, Logpush to R2 within the 25 GB internal allowance, webhook alerts, Terraform, and Workers Builds with Previews. Cloudflare now lets one user create up to five Free accounts, which is a natural way to keep staging apart from production ([extra accounts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-08-04-free-dashboard-button/)); whether spreading production load across accounts to multiply allowances is acceptable is a terms question the sources do not settle.

A native or mobile app does not change the backend, because it calls the same Worker API, but three details differ. Turnstile has no native SDK and must run in a WebView page ([Turnstile mobile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/mobile-implementation/)). Bot Fight Mode is a zone-wide switch with no exceptions and may challenge API and mobile traffic, so an app whose clients cannot pass a challenge should leave it off and rely on WAF rules, Turnstile and rate limits ([Bot Fight Mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/get-started/bot-fight-mode/)). RealtimeKit's SDKs cover Web, Android, iOS and React Native but its pricing is unresolved, so peer-to-peer WebRTC with Cloudflare TURN is the safer free route ([RealtimeKit pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/realtimekit/pricing/)). My recommendation is that a native app call its own Worker rather than embed Cloudflare API tokens, even though Workers AI offers a REST path.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes the framing of the challenge. "Every free thing" is the wrong unit, because Cloudflare's free tier is a system of daily and monthly budgets rather than a list of products, and the budgets that matter are small and tightly coupled: one Worker-request pool shared by HTTP traffic, Pages Functions, Workflows and Cron, 1,000 KV writes, 10,000 Queue operations and 10,000 Neurons. Unlimited appears in only a handful of places (static assets, DNS queries, DDoS mitigation, STUN and SFU ingress, inbound Email Routing and Turnstile challenges), and those are where a free build should push as much traffic as it can. The corollary is that the best zero-dollar build is the one that spends the fewest budgeted operations per user, not the one that switches on the most products, and that the honest definition of success is every standing free product whose allowance fits the app, plus a domain, a card on file for four products and a calendar of dates to re-check.

The second insight is directional. Over the past thirteen months Cloudflare added Queues to the Free plan, extended zone analytics to 31 days, opened Logpush and extra Free accounts, and made dashboard SSO free, while it paywalled frontier models, began enforcing D1 limits, converted betas into metered billing and scheduled a rewrite of the logging allowance. The durable core is the older, simpler products, and the newest ones arrive with expiry clocks, so a build should keep beta dependencies such as Secrets Store, LoRA, Traces, Mesh and Cloud Connector behind fallbacks and re-check four moments: 1 November, 1 December, the Q4 spending caps and early 2027. What remains unknown is less the headline allowances than their edges: what Queues, Workflows, Vectorize and Analytics Engine do at their caps, whether R2 truly demands a card, and how many zones a Free account can hold. Each is a short dashboard check that settles a design decision before it becomes an outage.
