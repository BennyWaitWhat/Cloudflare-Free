> Method: primary evidence is the raw markdown of developers.cloudflare.com pages (fetched 2026-10-14 via the `/index.md` versions, so no summariser in the loop) plus Cloudflare changelog posts and one Cloudflare blog post. Each page's own "Last updated" date is given where it matters. No Cloudflare resources were created or changed.
> Label legend used in "Inferences": FREE-FOREVER = permanent allowance on Workers Free; FREE-BETA = free only while in beta; TRIAL = trial or credits; PAID-ONLY = no free tier. No product in this scope is a trial/credit offer.

> Cross-cutting rule that is documented only for Durable Objects: on Workers Free, exceeding a free limit makes further operations of that type fail until the daily reset. Queues, Workflows and Analytics Engine do not document their Free-limit behavior (see Gaps).

## Key question 1: Durable Objects on Workers Free (SQLite-only? allowances, per-object limits, alarms, WebSockets/Hibernation, PITR, behavior at limit)

### Takeaway
Durable Objects are on Workers Free (since 2025-04-07) only as SQLite-backed classes. The free allowance is 100,000 requests/day, 13,000 GB-s duration/day, 5 million SQL rows read/day, 100,000 rows written/day and 5 GB total SQL storage; daily limits reset at 00:00 UTC and once one is exceeded, further operations of that type fail with an error (no overage billing on Free). The key-value backend is Paid-only and, since 2026-07-09, accounts that do not already have one cannot create one.

#### Plan availability, backend and history
- Durable Objects are available on both Workers Free and Workers Paid. Free: only SQLite-backed DOs. Paid: SQLite-backed, plus the key-value backend only for accounts that already have a key-value-backed namespace. To downgrade Paid -> Free you must first delete all key-value-backed namespaces. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/) (page last updated 2026-09-30); [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/) (last updated 2026-06-01)
- Free-plan launch, 2025-04-07: "Durable Objects can now be used with zero commitment on the Workers Free plan"; free-plan limits apply to compute and storage, and every Worker request can call a Durable Object. — [Changelog 2025-04-07](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-durable-objects-free-tier/)
- Cloudflare's launch blog (2025-04-07) says "Until today, Durable Objects was only accessible as part of our paid plans", "Durable Objects free tier can only access SQLite storage", SQLite storage went GA with a 10 GB SQLite database per Durable Object (public beta had been Sep 2024), and it lists the same free limits as today (100,000 requests/day, 13,000 GB-s/day, 5 million rows read/day, 100,000 rows written/day, 5 GB total). — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/building-ai-agents-with-mcp-authn-authz-and-durable-objects/); [Changelog: SQLite GA](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-sqlite-in-durable-objects-ga/)
- 2026-07-09: accounts without an existing KV-backed namespace can no longer create one (a `new_classes` migration fails; use `new_sqlite_classes`). "The Workers Free plan has only ever supported SQLite-backed Durable Objects." Accounts with an existing KV namespace can still create more "for now"; a migration path for existing KV-backed objects is signalled. — [Changelog 2026-07-09](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-07-09-restrict-new-kv-backed-namespaces/)
- SQLite-backed DOs have feature parity with the key-value backend (including the key-value storage API) and also support relational SQL and point-in-time recovery for the past 30 days. — [Changelog 2026-07-09](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-07-09-restrict-new-kv-backed-namespaces/)
- Key-value-backend storage pricing (read/write request units, delete requests, 1 GB stored) is listed for the Paid plan only. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- Rows read/written included limits and rates "match D1 pricing". — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)

#### Behavior at the limit
- On Workers Free: "If you exceed any one of the free tier limits, further operations of that type will fail with an error." Daily free limits reset at 00:00 UTC. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- SQLite storage billing (Paid only): target date 2026-01-07 (no earlier); "Developers on the Workers Free plan will not be charged"; compute billing for SQLite DOs has been active since the 2024 beta. — [Changelog 2025-12-12](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-12-12-durable-objects-sqlite-storage-billing/)
- A SQLite-backed DO that reaches its storage cap fails writes (INSERT/UPDATE/`put()`/`sql.exec`) with `database or disk is full: SQLITE_FULL`; reads and DELETEs still work. The FAQ states the cap as "10 GB on Workers Paid, or 1 GB on the Free plan". — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)
- Overload: each object is single-threaded with a soft limit of about 1,000 requests/second; excess requests are queued, then fail with "Durable Object is overloaded" errors (variants: too many requests queued, too much data queued, queued too long, too many requests within a 10-second window), which carry an `.overloaded` property. — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/); [DO troubleshooting](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/observability/troubleshooting/)
- The Workers-level Free cap also applies to the Worker that fronts a DO: 100,000 Worker requests/day, resetting midnight UTC, after which Cloudflare returns Error 1027 (route can fail open or closed). — [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) (last updated 2026-10-12)

#### How usage is counted (to stay under the free caps)
- HTTP request = 1 request. Each RPC session = 1 request; every RPC method call on a stub starts its own session; calls on `RpcTarget` stubs returned by that call share the session and are not billed separately. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- Creating a WebSocket connection = 1 request. Outgoing WebSocket messages and incoming protocol pings are not charged. For billing only, incoming WebSocket messages use a 20:1 ratio (100 messages = 5 requests); metrics show actual usage. Alarm invocations count as requests. Messages answered by `setWebSocketAutoResponse()` add no wall-clock time and are not charged. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- Duration = wall-clock time while an object is active, or idle but not eligible to hibernate; shared across concurrent requests on one object; every object is billed for a full 128 MB regardless of use, even when objects share an isolate. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- KV-style methods (`get/put/delete/list`) on SQLite-backed DOs are billed as rows read/written; `setAlarm()` = 1 row written; deletes count as rows written. An empty SQLite database uses about 12 KB; internal metadata written to a SQLite-backed object counts toward storage until `deleteAll()`. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- 2026-10-01: pending I/O (service-binding calls, DO-to-DO RPC/fetch, `waitUntil()` promises, timers) now keeps an object from idle eviction without a connected client (default for compatibility date 2026-10-01 or later; earlier dates can opt in); each operation is protected for up to 15 minutes and duration charges continue meanwhile. Outbound `connect()` sockets and outbound WebSockets do the same (2026-06-19). — [Changelog 2026-10-01](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-01-pending-io-keep-alive/); [DO lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/); [DO changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/durable-objects/)

#### WebSocket Hibernation and billing
- Calling `accept()` on a WebSocket incurs duration charges for the entire time the connection is open; the WebSocket Hibernation API avoids duration charges once event handlers finish. Idle objects eligible to hibernate are not billed for duration, even before the runtime actually hibernates them. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- Lifecycle: an eligible idle object hibernates after 10 seconds of inactivity (hibernated objects keep WebSocket clients connected); a non-hibernateable idle object is evicted after 70-140 seconds. Hibernation requires no `setTimeout`/`setInterval` callbacks and no in-flight request; a wake-up re-runs the constructor. — [DO lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/)
- The Hibernation API permits at most 32,768 WebSocket connections per Durable Object (CPU/memory may lower the practical figure); tags: max 10 per socket, 256 chars each. — [DO State API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/state/)
- `serializeAttachment` max serialized size is 16,384 bytes; hibernation only applies when the DO is the WebSocket server (outgoing sockets never hibernate); alarms, incoming requests and `setTimeout`/`setInterval` prevent hibernation. — [DO WebSockets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/)
- Received WebSocket message size limit is 32 MiB (raised from 1 MiB on 2025-10-31). — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/); [Changelog 2025-10-31](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-10-31-increased-websocket-message-size-limit/)
- Docs' own Paid pricing examples (each includes the $5/month minimum): 100 objects x 100 Hibernatable WebSockets at one message/min per client (about 1 s of JS execution per object per minute) = $20.65/month, versus $142.95 (100 objects x 50 sockets, ~1 message/min/client for 8 hours/day, with each object billed as continuously active for those 8 hours = 11.06M GB-s) and $419.30 (100 objects x 1 socket, one message/second, active all month = 33.2M GB-s). The docs do not call examples 2-3 "non-hibernating", but their duration math shows no hibernation savings. — [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)

#### Per-account and per-object limits
- Classes per account: 100 (Free) / 500 (Paid), identical to the Workers script limit. Objects: unlimited per account or class. Storage per class: unlimited. Storage per account: 5 GB (Free) / unlimited (Paid); footnote says Free accounts are limited to 5 GB total DO storage (1 GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes). — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)
- Storage per DO: table says 10 GB; the FAQ says 1 GB on the Free plan. The page is internally inconsistent about the Free per-object figure. — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)
- SQL limits: 100 columns/table; unlimited rows/table (bounded by storage); string/BLOB/row 2 MB; statement length 100 KB; 100 bound parameters/query; 32 arguments/SQL function; LIKE/GLOB pattern 50 bytes; key + value combined <= 2 MB. — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)
- CPU: the DO limits table lists 30 s default per request (configurable to 5 min via `limits.cpu_ms`; each incoming HTTP request or WebSocket message resets the budget) with no Free/Paid split, but the same page says DOs are Workers so "Workers Limits apply according to your Workers plan"; the Workers limits page gives Workers Free 10 ms CPU per HTTP request (Paid 5 min, default 30 s). Memory 128 MB (both plans); 6 simultaneous outgoing connections per request. — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/); [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)
- Wall time: alarm handlers 15 minutes; DO RPC/HTTP unlimited while the caller stays connected. — [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)

#### Alarms (usable as a per-object scheduler)
- One alarm per DO at a time (`setAlarm()` overrides); at-least-once execution; automatic retries with exponential backoff starting at 2 s, up to 6 retries; many events can be multiplexed by keeping a schedule in storage and re-arming one alarm; unlimited DOs can each hold an alarm (unlike a limited number of Cron Triggers). — [DO alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/)
- `ctx.abort({ retryAlarm: false })` stops an interrupted alarm retrying (2026-08-25); `deleteAll()` also deletes the alarm for compatibility date 2026-02-24 or later. — [Changelog 2026-08-25](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-08-25-durable-object-alarm-abort-no-retry/); [Changelog 2026-02-24](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-24-deleteall-deletes-alarms/)
- For comparison, Workers Free allows 5 Cron Triggers per account (Paid 250). — [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)

#### KV-style API vs SQL API, point-in-time recovery, tooling
- SQLite-backed objects expose the SQL API (`ctx.storage.sql.exec`), a synchronous KV API (`ctx.storage.kv`), the asynchronous KV API and the PITR API; KV-backed objects only have the asynchronous KV API. — [SQLite storage API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/)
- PITR restores the embedded SQLite database (SQL and KV data) to any point in the past 30 days using bookmarks (`getCurrentBookmark`, `getBookmarkForTime`, `onNextSessionRestoreBookmark` followed by `ctx.abort()`); the restore can itself be undone via the returned bookmark. No plan restriction is stated. — [SQLite storage API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/)
- Data Studio (beta, 2025-10-16): dashboard view/edit of SQLite-backed DO storage; needs at least the Workers Platform Admin role; queries are audit-logged and "incur normal usage billing". — [Changelog 2025-10-16](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-10-16-durable-objects-data-studio/)

### Inferences
- Label: FREE-FOREVER (daily-reset allowance), SQLite-backed only. Status: GA (SQLite storage GA 2025-04-07); Data Studio is beta. Paid-only: key-value backend (legacy), unlimited account storage, 500 classes, monthly pooled allowances with overage.
- Prerequisites: docs call Free use "zero commitment"; no payment method, Workers Paid plan or Cloudflare-hosted zone is required by anything quoted above (a Worker on workers.dev can bind a DO). Treat "no card, no zone" as an inference, not a quoted fact.
- Hitting any one cap (requests, duration, rows read, rows written) breaks only that type of operation until 00:00 UTC. Rows written at 100,000/day is the tightest cap for write-heavy apps (each KV `put`, each INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE and each `setAlarm` costs rows); hibernatable WebSockets and avoiding polling protect the duration/request caps.
- If Workers plan limits really apply to DOs on Free, the effective CPU per invocation is about 10 ms (not 30 s); size handlers accordingly.
- Use idea for website + app: one SQLite-backed DO per chat room / document / user session for real-time presence, rate limiting and consistent counters, with hibernatable WebSockets so idle rooms cost no duration and an alarm for per-room cleanup.
- Superseded: WebSocket message cap 1 MiB -> 32 MiB (2025-10-31); creating KV-backed namespaces is now restricted (2026-07-09); SQLite storage billing (Paid) began on/after 2026-01-07.

### Gaps
- The Free-plan CPU time per DO invocation is not stated explicitly (30 s default in the DO table vs Workers Free 10 ms).
- Free per-object storage is contradictory in the docs (10 GB table vs 1 GB FAQ); only the 5 GB account total is consistent.
- No separate documented cap on "namespaces" beyond the 100-class limit (a namespace corresponds to a class binding; this equivalence is my inference).
- Whether PITR or Data Studio have any Free-plan restriction is not stated either way.

## Key question 2: Workflows on Workers Free (instances, steps, concurrency, creation rate, payloads, sleep/wait, retention, billing)

### Takeaway
Workflows (GA since 2025-04-07; "Are Workflows available on the Workers Free plan? Yes.") has on Free 100,000 requests/day shared with Workers, 10 ms CPU per invocation, 3,000 steps/day and 1 GB-month storage. Free limits: 1,024 steps per Workflow, 100 concurrent running instances, 100 creations/second, 100,000 queued instances, 100 MB state per instance, 3-day retention of completed state. Step and storage billing was set to start no earlier than 2026-08-10 on Paid, and Free "will not be charged beyond the included amounts".

#### Pricing and billing mechanics (page last updated 2026-09-21)
- Workflows is included in both the Free and Paid Workers plans; it uses Workers Standard pricing for CPU and requests and is billed on four dimensions: CPU time, requests (invocations), storage (GB persisted) and steps. — [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)
- Free: requests 100,000/day (shared with Workers requests), CPU 10 ms/invocation, storage 1 GB-month, steps 3,000/day. Paid: 10M requests/month then $0.30/M; 30M CPU-ms/month then $0.02/M ms; 1 GB-month then $0.20/GB-month; 500,000 steps/month then $0.80 per additional 100,000. — [Workers pricing, Workflows section](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/); [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)
- A request = starting an instance (Workers API, Wrangler or REST); steps and subrequests are not requests. Step count excludes rollback handlers and retries. Idle waiting (API response, `step.sleep`) costs no CPU time. — [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)
- Storage is the average of each day's peak over a 30-day period, across running, errored, sleeping and completed instances; deleting instances frees storage (may take minutes to reflect). — [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)
- Per-step billing announced 2026-07-07; step and storage billing "no earlier than August 10, 2026" (Paid); "Developers on the Workers Free plan will not be charged for steps or storage beyond the included amounts"; requests and CPU billing had been active since the initial beta. A step includes `sleep` and `waitForEvent` operations. — [Changelog 2026-07-07](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-07-07-workflows-billing-updates/)
- On Free, an instance that tries to store state after hitting the storage limit throws an error. The pricing page does not say what happens when Free requests, CPU or steps are exhausted. — [Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/)

#### Limits detail (page last updated 2026-09-21)
- Max steps per Workflow: Free 1,024; Paid 10,000 default, configurable to 25,000 (raised 2026-03-03). `step.sleep` does not count as a step toward this limit. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/); [Changelog list](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/workflows/)
- Concurrent instances (only `running` ones count; `waiting` instances from `step.sleep`, retry waits or `step.waitForEvent` do not): Free 100; Paid 50,000 (raised from 10,000 on 2026-04-15). Creation rate: Free 100/s; Paid 300/s per account and 100/s per Workflow; exceeding it returns HTTP 429. Queued instances: Free 100,000; Paid 2,000,000. The 2026-04-15 increases apply to Workers Paid only. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/); [Changelog 2026-04-15](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-04-15-workflows-limits-raised/)
- Payloads: non-stream `step.do()` result 1 MiB (JS steps may return a `ReadableStream<Uint8Array>` for larger output, still counted toward storage); event payload 1 MiB; persisted state per instance Free 100 MB / Paid 1 GB. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)
- Time: `step.sleep` max 365 days; wall clock per step unlimited; compute time per step Free 10 ms / Paid 30 s default (up to 5 min CPU); max retries per step 10,000. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)
- Retention of completed state: Free 3 days (default and limit); Paid max 30 days. Configurable via `default_retention` or per-instance `retention`; the Free plan keeps its 3-day default and limit. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/); [Changelog 2026-09-10](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-10-paid-retention-default/)
- Subrequests: Free 50 external plus 1,000 to Cloudflare services per invocation; Paid 10,000 default, up to 10 million. Workflow executions: Free 100,000/day shared with the Workers daily limit; Paid unlimited. Script size 3 MB (Free) / 10 MB (Paid); 100 (Free) / 500 (Paid) scripts; name <= 64 chars; instance ID <= 100 chars. Workflows cannot be deployed to Workers for Platforms namespaces. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)
- Errors at limits: CPU overrun throws "Worker exceeded CPU time limit"; subrequest overrun throws "Too many subrequests"; creation-rate overrun returns HTTP 429. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)
- Doc inconsistency: the prose about `waiting` instances still says "10,000 concurrent instance limit" while the table shows 50,000 for Paid. — [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)

#### waitForEvent, sleep and schedules
- `step.waitForEvent(name, { type, timeout })`: `type` up to 100 characters; default timeout 24 hours; timeout may be between 1 second and 365 days; on timeout the Workflow throws and the instance fails unless the call is wrapped in `try/catch`; events are delivered with `instance.sendEvent({ type, payload })`. — [Workflows Workers API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/workers-api/); [Events and parameters](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/events-and-parameters/)
- Docs recommend step timeouts of 30 minutes or less, using `waitForEvent` for longer waits, and `createBatch()` to avoid creation-rate limits. — [Rules of Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/rules-of-workflows/)
- Cron-started instances: a `schedules` array on the Workflow binding (max 100 cron expressions per account, 256 chars each) creates an instance per matching cron run, with no separate `scheduled` handler; the docs state no plan restriction on `schedules` itself. Only on Workers Paid do scheduled instances get up to one hour per firing without using a concurrency slot. — [Trigger Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/trigger-workflows/); [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/); [Changelog 2026-06-02](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-06-02-cron-workflows/)

#### Status and timeline
- GA 2025-04-07 (added `waitForEvent`/`sendEvent`, concurrency 4,500, Vitest support). — [Changelog 2025-04-07](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-workflows-ga/)
- Later: concurrency 4,500 -> 10,000 and creation 100 per 10 s -> 100/s (2025-10-31, Paid) -> 50,000 and 300/s (2026-04-15, Paid); queued instances 1M -> 2M; Paid steps 1,024 -> up to 25,000 (2026-03-03); Python Workflows beta (2025-08-22); dashboard visualizer beta (2026-02-04); rollbacks (2026-06-05); `.subscribe()` instance-event streaming (2026-09-15); batch delete up to 100 instances (2026-09-17); `createBatch()` up to 100 instances per call (2026-10-08). — [Workflows changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/workflows/)

### Inferences
- Label: FREE-FOREVER (3,000 steps/day, shared 100,000 requests/day, 1 GB-month storage). Status: GA (Python Workflows and the dashboard visualizer are beta). Paid-only: more than 1,024 steps, more than 100 concurrent instances, 30-day retention, 5-minute CPU per step, 1 GB state, large subrequest budgets, Paid cron concurrency budget.
- Prerequisites: Workers Free only; the sources quoted state no payment-method or zone requirement.
- Free is tight on CPU (10 ms per invocation) so steps must be I/O-bound (calls to D1/KV/Queues/external APIs) rather than compute-heavy; 3,000 steps/day caps volume (about 300 runs/day of a 10-step Workflow), and every instance creation also draws on the shared 100,000 requests/day.
- Use idea: a durable signup / order / content-publish flow with retries, an email send and a human-approval `waitForEvent` (up to 365 days), plus a cron-scheduled instance for nightly cleanup.
- Superseded figures: concurrency 4,500 and 10,000; creation rate 100 per 10 s; 1,024 steps for Paid; Paid 30-day default retention (now 7 days for new Workflows).

### Gaps
- Behavior when the Free daily steps (3,000) or requests (100,000) are exhausted is not documented for Workflows (the Workers-level Free request cap returns Error 1027, which may or may not govern instance creation).
- No documented `sendEvent` rate limit; no documented cap on total stored instances beyond the 100,000 queued-instance and storage limits.

## Key question 3: Queues on Workers Free (availability, operations, limits, retention, DLQ, pull/HTTP, event subscriptions)

### Takeaway
Queues has been on Workers Free since 2026-02-04: up to 10,000 queues and 10,000 operations per day (reads + writes + deletes, per 64 KB chunk), all features including unlimited event subscriptions, with message retention fixed at 24 hours (Paid: up to 14 days). All other limits equal Paid. The docs do not say what happens when the 10,000 daily operations are used up.

### Cited Findings
#### At a glance
| Item | Workers Free | Workers Paid | Source |

#### Availability and pricing (pages last updated 2026-04-21)
- 2026-02-04: "Cloudflare Queues is now part of the Workers free plan": up to 10,000 queues to Workers or HTTP pull consumers; 10,000 operations per day across reads, writes and deletes; all features including unlimited event subscriptions; maximum retention on Free is 24 hours rather than 14 days. — [Changelog 2026-02-04](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-04-queues-free-plan/)
- The Queues overview says "Available on Free and Paid plans". — [Queues overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/)
- Operation = each 64 KB written, read or deleted (KB = 1,000 bytes; ~100 bytes of metadata per message); counted per message not per batch; a normal delivery = 3 operations (write, read, delete); each retry costs a read; a message written to a Dead Letter Queue costs a write per 64 KB chunk (so a message retried 3 times then dead-lettered costs 5 reads); expired-unread messages cost only write + delete; no egress or bandwidth charges. — [Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/)

#### Limits (identical on Free and Paid except retention)
- Queues 10,000/account; message size 128 KB; message retries up to 100 (default `max_retries` 3); max consumer batch 100 messages (`max_batch_size` default 10); `sendBatch` max 100 messages or 256 KB; max batch wait 60 s (default 5 s); per-queue throughput 5,000 messages/s (`send()`/`sendBatch()` throw `Too Many Requests` above it); per-queue backlog 25 GB (`Storage Limit Exceeded` error); concurrent consumer invocations 250 (push-based only; consumers autoscale, `max_concurrency` 1-250); consumer wall time 15 minutes; consumer CPU 30 s default up to 5 minutes; `visibilityTimeout` (pull) up to 12 hours; `delaySeconds` up to 24 hours. — [Queues limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/limits/); [Batching, retries and delays](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/); [Consumer concurrency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/consumer-concurrency/)
- The limits page says retention is "non-configurable at 24 hours for the Workers Free plan" and messages reaching maximum retention are deleted. — [Queues limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/limits/)
- Pull (HTTP) consumers: need an API token with `queues_read` and `queues_write`; short polling; `batch_size` default 5 (max 100); `visibility_timeout` default 30 s (max 12 hours); pull throughput raised to 5,000 messages/s per queue on 2025-04-17 (from a shared 1,200 requests per 5 minutes). — [Pull consumers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/pull-consumers/); [Changelog 2025-04-17](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-17-pull-consumer-limits/)
- Publishing over HTTP from any language is supported (2025-05-09) using an API token with Queues Edit permission. — [Changelog 2025-05-09](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-05-09-publish-to-queues-via-http/)
- Dead Letter Queues: messages that exhaust retries are deleted, or written to the DLQ if one is configured; the DLQ must share the source queue's jurisdiction; a DLQ with no consumer keeps messages four days (stated in the DLQ doc; not reconciled with the 24-hour Free retention). — [Batching, retries and delays](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/); [Dead Letter Queues](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/dead-letter-queues/)
- Other additions: pause/purge APIs (2025-03-27), configurable retention 60 s - 14 days (2025-02-14, Paid only in effect), realtime backlog metrics (2026-04-28), jurisdiction-restricted queues for EU/US/FedRAMP-Moderate set only at creation (2026-10-12; no plan restriction stated). — [Queues changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/queues/); [Changelog 2026-10-12](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-18-queues-jurisdictions/)

#### Event subscriptions from other Cloudflare products (included on Free)
- Event subscriptions let Cloudflare products publish structured events to a queue consumed by Workers or HTTP pull; created from the queue's Subscriptions tab or `wrangler queues subscription create <queue> --source <type> --events <list>`. Launched 2025-08-19. — [Event subscriptions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/event-subscriptions/); [Manage subscriptions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/event-subscriptions/manage-event-subscriptions/); [Changelog 2025-08-19](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-08-19-event-subscriptions/)
- Current sources and events (Events & schemas page): Access (`application.created/deleted`); Artifacts (`repo.created/deleted/forked/imported`, `pushed`, `cloned`, `fetched`, `token.created/revoked`); Browser Run (`crawl.started/updated/finished`); Email Sending (`message.delivered/deferred/bounced/failed/rejected/complained`); R2 (`bucket.created`, `bucket.deleted`); Super Slurper (`job.started/paused/resumed/completed/aborted`, `job.object.migrated`); Vectorize (`index.created/deleted`); Workers AI (`batch.queued/succeeded/failed`); Workers Builds (`build.started/failed/canceled/succeeded`); Workers KV (`namespace.created/deleted`); Workflows (`instance.queued/started/paused/errored/terminated/completed`). — [Events & schemas](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/event-subscriptions/events-schemas/)
- Source additions over time: Workers Builds notifications (2026-01-09), Artifacts (2026-05-19), Email Sending (2026-07-15; Email Routing events not included per the post summary), Browser Run crawl events (2026-09-25). — [Queues changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/queues/)
- Free: the 2026-02-04 post says Free includes "unlimited event subscriptions"; the pricing docs list no separate charge for subscriptions, so delivered events are ordinary queue messages consuming operations. — [Changelog 2026-02-04](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-04-queues-free-plan/); [Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/)
- R2 object-level "event notifications" are a separate R2 feature: rules (up to 100 per bucket, optional prefix/suffix filters) send `object-create` and `object-delete` messages to a queue. Prerequisites: an existing R2 bucket, an existing queue, and a consumer Worker or HTTP pull. The R2 pricing page lists no separate event-notification charge (keyword check). — [R2 event notifications](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/event-notifications/); [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)

### Inferences
- Label: FREE-FOREVER (10,000 operations/day since 2026-02-04). Status: GA (overview shows no beta label). Paid-only: retention beyond 24 hours, anything above 10,000 operations/day (Paid pools 1M/month with $0.40/M overage).
- At 3 operations per message, 10,000 operations/day is only about 3,300 end-to-end messages/day (fewer with retries, DLQ writes or messages over 64 KB); event subscriptions and R2 notifications share that budget, so a noisy source can exhaust it.
- Prerequisites: Workers Free; no payment method stated. One tutorial still says "You need a Workers Paid plan to enable Queues" (and also "A limited version of Queues is also available on the Workers Free plan"), which contradicts the 2026-02-04 post and looks stale. — [Handle rate limits tutorial](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/tutorials/handle-rate-limits/)
- Use idea: buffer contact-form submissions, webhooks and image-processing jobs through a queue into a consumer Worker with a DLQ; subscribe to Workers Builds events for deploy notifications or to R2 object-created notifications for upload processing.
- Superseded: "Queues needs Workers Paid" (before 2026-02-04); pull-consumer cap of 1,200 requests/5 min replaced by 5,000 messages/s (2025-04-17); fixed 4-day retention replaced by configurable 60 s - 14 days (2025-02-14, Paid).

### Gaps
- What happens when Free's 10,000 daily operations are exceeded (errors, throttling, dropped messages) is not stated on the pricing, limits or announcement pages; no Queues equivalent of the D1 enforcement notice was found.
- Whether queue consumer invocations count toward the Workers Free 100,000 requests/day cap is not stated in the pages read.
- Queues GA date not sourced (no beta label shown).

## Key question 4: Pipelines (now Basin Pipelines) — availability on Free, status, pricing, limits, relationship to R2 Data Catalog (now Basin Catalog)

## Key question 5: Workers Analytics Engine (free data points/day, read queries/day, retention, cardinality/blob limits, SQL API on Free)

### Takeaway
The pricing page lists Workers Free at 100,000 data points written per day and 10,000 read queries per day (Paid: 10M writes/month and 1M queries/month included), but Workers Analytics Engine is not billed on any plan yet ("in the coming months", no date). Data is kept for three months; the SQL API works with an Account Analytics Read token. Behavior past the free caps is not documented.

### Cited Findings
- Pricing (last updated 2026-04-23): Workers Paid 10 million data points written/month then $0.25/million and 1 million read queries/month then $1.00/million; Workers Free 100,000 data points written/day and 10,000 read queries/day. "Currently, you will not be billed for your use of Workers Analytics Engine"; prices are shared in advance of billing "in the coming months"; no start date. — [AE pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/pricing/)
- Counting: each `writeDataPoint()` call = 1 data point whatever its dimensions or cardinality; each POST to the SQL API = 1 read query whatever its complexity. — [AE pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/pricing/)
- Write limits: up to 20 blobs, 20 doubles and 1 index per `writeDataPoint`; total blob size 16 KB per data point (raised from 5 KB on 2025-06-20); each index <= 96 bytes; max 250 data points per Worker invocation; retention three months (longer retention is requested via the #analytics-engine Discord channel). — [AE limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/limits/) (last updated 2026-04-23); [Changelog 2025-06-20](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-06-20-increased-blob-size-limits-in-Workers-Analytics/)
- Cardinality: the product is described as providing "unlimited-cardinality analytics at scale"; no cardinality limit is listed on the limits page, and no plan-specific differences are stated. — [AE overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/); [AE limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/limits/)
- Setup: an `analytics_engine_datasets` binding in the Wrangler config (the dataset is auto-created on first write); `writeDataPoint()` needs no `await`; only a single index is accepted (more than one means the data point is not recorded). — [AE get started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/get-started/)
- Querying: `POST https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/accounts/<account_id>/analytics_engine/sql` with a bearer token holding Account > Account Analytics > Read; the GraphQL API and Grafana can also read it. Data may be downsampled on write and on read (the index is the sampling key), exposed via `_sample_interval`. — [AE SQL API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/sql-api/) (last updated 2026-10-02); [AE get started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/get-started/)
- A separate Analytics SQL API can query the same data as `events.analyticsEngine.<DATASET>` (adaptively sampled; account tag required). — [Analytics SQL API datasets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/sql-api/datasets/)
- Product changelog shows only SQL feature additions (functions 2025-10-02 and 2025-11-12, HAVING/LIKE 2026-01-07) and no billing-start announcement as of 2026-10-14. — [AE changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/workers-analytics-engine/)

### Inferences
- Label: FREE-FOREVER allowance on paper for Workers Free (100,000 writes/day, 10,000 queries/day), currently also unmetered for every plan (pre-billing). Status: the docs label neither beta nor GA. Paid-only: monthly pooled quotas with overage once billing starts.
- Prerequisites: no zone, domain or payment method is mentioned; a Worker binding plus an API token is all that is needed.
- Because enforcement behavior is undocumented, plan around the stated Free caps anyway; one data point per request means roughly 100,000 logged requests/day, and every dashboard refresh that POSTs SQL spends one of 10,000 daily queries (so cache results).
- Use idea: write one data point per page view or API call (route, country, status, latency) and chart it from a small admin page or Grafana via the SQL API.

### Gaps
- What happens when the Free daily caps are exceeded (dropped writes, rejected queries) is not documented.
- No billing start date; no documented SQL API rate limit beyond the 10,000/day figure.

## Key question 6: Other related stateful / orchestration / messaging / event features discovered

### Takeaway
Related pieces for a free build are Durable Object alarms (per-object scheduler), Workflows `schedules` (cron-started durable workflows), Queues `delaySeconds` and event subscriptions, R2 object event notifications into Queues, and Logpush into Basin Pipelines (paid). Cron Triggers themselves belong to the Workers researcher.

### Cited Findings
- Workflows can be scheduled from the binding (`schedules`, 2026-06-02; max 100 cron expressions per account); DO alarms give one alarm per object with at-least-once retries (2 s exponential backoff, up to 6 retries) and a 15-minute handler. — [Trigger Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/trigger-workflows/); [DO alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/)
- Workers Free allows 5 Cron Triggers per account (Paid 250). — [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)
- Queues can delay a message up to 24 hours (`delaySeconds`, also a per-queue delivery delay). — [Queues limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/limits/); [Batching, retries and delays](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/)
- Workflows is itself an event source for Queues event subscriptions, and instance events can also be streamed with `.subscribe()` (2026-09-15). — [Events & schemas](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/event-subscriptions/events-schemas/); [Workflows changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/workflows/)
- Basin Pipelines accepts Logpush as a stream source (2026-04-20). — [Basin Pipelines changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/basin-pipelines/)
- Free-tier enforcement precedent from the same platform: from 2026-09-01, D1 queries on Workers Free fail once the daily row read/write limits are exceeded, until midnight UTC (email alert on reaching the limit). — [Changelog 2026-09-01](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-01-d1-free-tier-limit-enforcement/)
- Pay-as-you-go accounts (including Queues and Durable Objects users) got billable-usage and budget alerts in product sidebars on 2026-06-04; Enterprise contract accounts are not yet supported. — [Queues changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/queues/)

### Inferences
- Combined free pattern: Worker -> Queue (buffer, DLQ) -> consumer Worker -> Durable Object (per-entity state, alarms) and/or a Workflow (multi-step, human approval, cron), with Workers Analytics Engine for metrics. Each service has its own free pool, but Worker, Workflow-creation and Queue-consumer traffic may all draw on the shared Workers Free 100,000 requests/day.
- Free-tier design rule: only Durable Objects documents "fail until the daily reset"; for Queues, Workflows and Analytics Engine, build graceful degradation (catch send errors, queue-less fallbacks) because limit behavior is undocumented.

### Gaps
- No further stateful/messaging/orchestration product with a free tier was found in this area beyond those covered; Artifacts, Browser Run, Email Sending and Vectorize appear only as event sources and are covered by other researchers.
